# Educator Guide — Make Ten Adventure

## Purpose and prerequisites

This short lesson gives a learner around age 6 practice connecting numerals with quantities from 0–10, composing 10, and representing addition/subtraction within 10. Prerequisite: informal counting of small groups. It is practice, not a developmental or diagnostic assessment.

Suggested session: 8–12 minutes, with an adult nearby. Stopping early is fine.

## Learning objectives

The learner gets opportunities to:
1. build a visible quantity for a numeral, including 0 and 10;
2. see at least five different number bonds that compose 10, including 0+10 and 5+5;
3. model short addition and subtraction stories within 10 before confirming an equation.

## Walkthrough

### 1. Build a quantity
Ask the learner to tap the ten-frame until it matches the displayed numeral. “Watch example” demonstrates numeral 3 with three visible counters. “New target” cycles through 0, 1, 3, 5, 7, and 10.

### 2. Make ten
A starting part is blue. The learner chooses how many gold counters complete ten. The sequence includes 0, 1, 2, 5, 7, 8, and 10 as starting quantities. “Watch 7 + 3” gives one explicit worked model.

### 3. Story problems
Seven original situations include addition and subtraction. The learner can tap the picture model, choose an answer, request a hint, retry without penalty, and inspect the matching equation after a correct answer. The seventh item is a fresh “how many more make ten?” challenge.

## Differentiation and co-play

Two help levels are learner-selectable and are deliberately not labelled by ability. Level 1 names a strategy such as counting on or taking away. Level 2 gives a stronger numerical cue while still leaving the response to the learner.

For a learner who needs less visual load, the adult can stay on one activity, use a single number bond repeatedly, cover unused answer buttons with a hand or paper, and narrate one counter at a time. For more challenge, ask the learner to predict the answer before touching the frame or explain a second way to make 10.

Optional read-aloud is off by default and starts only after the read-aloud button is intentionally switched on. Every spoken prompt remains visible.

## Model limitations

Ten-frames and counters are simplified representations. They support sense-making but are not the only valid representation of number. The site does not infer understanding from free-form language or claim measured learning gains.

Progress exists only in page memory. Reloading or “Start again” clears it.

## Offline follow-up

Put 6 small objects on a table and ask, “How many more would make ten?” Repeat with another starting amount. Then tell a tiny add/take-away story and let the learner move the objects before writing or saying an equation.
